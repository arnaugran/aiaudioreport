Renting Serum 2 through Splice costs 75 cents more than buying it outright from Xfer Records, and Splice adds a 3-day free trial. That's the whole price difference, and it's small enough that the decision rests on something else: what you're left with if you stop paying halfway.

The short version is nothing you can keep. Until the last payment clears, you're paying for access, not for a share of a licence.

## The numbers, side by side

Read on 12 November 2026, in US dollars.

Splice's own help centre calls the outright price $249, and its product page says you'll pay "roughly the same amount" as buying direct at a non-discounted price. The arithmetic agrees. No interest, no setup fee, and you can pay off the remaining balance at any point to own it sooner.

## What cancelling actually does

This is the part the product page compresses into "Cancel any time", and it's the reason I wrote this piece. Splice's FAQ on the Serum 2 page says:

> "If you cancel your plan, you will keep access to your Serum 2 plan until the end of your current billing cycle. For so long as Splice offers the product, you may resume your plan and continue your payments."

Two things follow from that wording.

The first is that cancelling isn't a pause. Once the billing cycle ends, so does your access, and the months you've already paid don't turn into a partial licence. The FAQ is explicit about when ownership starts: "Once you've paid it off, you'll redeem a lifetime license and the plugin is yours forever." Before that point there's no licence to keep.

The second is the escape hatch, and it's conditional. You can come back and "continue your payments", which reads as picking up where you left off rather than starting again at month one. But that promise lasts only "for so long as Splice offers the product". If Serum 2 ever leaves Splice's catalogue while you're lapsed, the page gives you no route back in.

Compare that with Splice's sample plans, where I've written about credits [expiring 28 days after you cancel](/articles/splice-vs-loopcloud-credits/). Rent-to-own is billed separately from those plans, and Splice's Serum 2 FAQ says you don't need a sample subscription to rent or use the synth at all.

## The 3-day check-in

While you're renting, Serum 2 is tied to the Splice desktop app. Splice's help centre says to keep the latest version installed and "open it at least once every 3 days to keep your Serum 2 lease authorized."

That's fine on the laptop you use every day. It's a real problem on a studio machine you deliberately keep offline, or a computer you only open for a session every couple of weeks. The renting period is 25 months, so this isn't a short inconvenience.

Once it's paid off the tie goes away. You activate the lifetime copy through your Xfer Records account, no serial number involved, and Xfer offers an offline licence file for computers without internet.

## How many computers

Splice contradicts itself here. The FAQ on the Serum 2 product page says "You can install your copy of Serum 2 on up to 2 computers." Two help-centre articles, the Serum 2 FAQ and the one on installing across computers, both say four, and the second walks through freeing up an activation when you hit the limit.

The help-centre articles are the more detailed and more specific of the two, and both agree with each other. Still, if you need Serum 2 on more than two machines during the lease, I'd ask Splice support before starting the trial rather than relying on either page.

## If you're already renting the original Serum

You don't pay again. Splice's Serum 2 FAQ says anyone still renting Serum gets Serum 2 in the desktop app "at no extra cost", can run both for the rest of the lease, and owns both plugins when the original Serum is paid off. People who already paid Serum off get the Serum 2 upgrade free through their Xfer Records account. Presets made for the original Serum load in Serum 2; presets made in Serum 2 don't go the other way.

## Which one I'd pick

If you can pay $249 today, buy it from Xfer Records. It costs 75 cents less, the licence is yours on day one, and nothing about your studio has to check in with Splice every three days for two years.

Rent-to-own makes sense when $249 up front is the obstacle and $9.99 a month isn't. For that person it's a fair deal: no interest, a 3-day trial, and the option to clear the balance early once money comes in. Just treat it as a commitment to all 25 payments, because a cancellation at month 20 leaves you with the same number of licences as a cancellation at month one, which is none.
