Loopmasters' Dark Rollers pack costs €41.95 for the full download: 258 files, or about 16 cents each. A Loopcloud subscription can bring the per-sound cost lower, provided you use the points before the month ends.

That makes the subscription cheaper on paper. A pack is yours to keep and revisit whenever you like, which can matter more than the unit price.

## One pack is not one price

This is the part the product pages do not lead with. Loopmasters does not sell Dark Rollers as
a single item. It sells six, and you pick.

| What you buy | Price |
|---|---|
| Drum & Perc Wavs | €11.95 |
| Bass & Fx Wavs | €15.45 |
| Instrument & Vocal Wavs | €17.95 |
| One Shots | €17.95 |
| Wav Loops | €29.95 |
| ZIP (main), everything | €41.95 |

Two useful things fall out of that table.

If you want the whole pack, buy the ZIP and do not assemble it from parts. The three Wav
subsets add up to €45.35, and Wav Loops plus One Shots comes to €47.90. Both are more than the
€41.95 complete pack.

If you only want drums, the picture flips completely. Drum & Perc on its own is €11.95, which
is €30 less than the ZIP. Producers who buy packs for the drums and never open the synth loops
are routinely paying three and a half times what they need to.

## Against a subscription, in the same currency

Comparing these properly means staying in one currency, because I haven't verified an
exchange rate, and I haven't invented one. Loopcloud prices in euros, so that is the fair
comparison.

Loopcloud's Professional tier is €229.50 a year for 600 points a month, which works out at
about €0.0319 a point. A loop on Loopcloud usually costs two points, so call it **€0.064 a
loop**. I went through that arithmetic in detail in
[the points calculator](/articles/loopcloud-points-calculator/).

So the pack is around €0.16 a sound and the subscription is around €0.064. Buying outright
costs roughly two and a half times as much per sound.

Splice sits in the same territory but prices in dollars, at $0.108 a sample on Sounds billed
yearly and $0.083 on Creator. I haven't converted those, deliberately. See
[Is Splice Worth It?](/articles/is-splice-worth-it/) for that side.

## What the premium actually buys

Two and a half times more per sound sounds damning until you look at what the cheaper number
requires of you.

Subscription sounds are metered. You get an allowance, and on Splice unspent credits expire 28
days after you cancel. The low per sound price assumes you reliably use the allowance, every
month, indefinitely. Miss that and your real cost per sound rises fast, which is the whole
argument of the Splice piece.

A pack has none of that. Pay once, keep the files, stop thinking about it. There is no
allowance to burn down, no renewal date, nothing that expires if you take three months off.
For someone who makes music in bursts, €41.95 once is better value than €229.50 a year even
though the per sound maths says otherwise.

The other thing you buy is a decision already made. A pack is 258 files someone curated to go
together at 174 BPM. A subscription is three million files and a search box. If what you lack
is time rather than money, that curation is the product.

## The licence, which nobody reads

Loopmasters is unusually blunt here, and the wording is worth quoting rather than summarising.

On ownership: "you are NOT obtaining OWNERSHIP of the sounds - you are in fact purchasing a
LICENSE to use the sounds within your musical compositions". The capitals are theirs. "The
original producer of the sounds will always remain the OWNER of the sounds."

On what you may do: you may use the sounds "in combination with other sounds within your
musical compositions", and release those commercially "without payment of any further fees or
royalties". Their own example is a sample in a TV advert broadcast hundreds of times, where
you keep all the royalties.

Then the restriction that catches people:

> You cannot use the sounds in isolation (i.e. when not within musical compositions) as part
> of a commercially released project - for instance you cannot use the sounds to make another
> sample pack, or for sound effects on a website or multimedia production - unless as part of
> a composition with other sounds.

So a one shot dropped into a video as a UI click, or a riser used alone as a transition in a
commercial project, is outside the licence. Inside a track, it is fine. That distinction is
not obvious from the words "royalty free" printed on the product page.

Two smaller things. The DJ Mixtools series has its own agreement and those sounds cannot be
used to produce library music. And Loopmasters sells other labels' packs through its store,
where it says to check each label's agreement, with the general rule being royalty free "with
certain exceptions".

## Which to buy

Subscribe if you open a DAW most weeks and you are hunting for sounds rather than for a
specific record's worth of them. The per sound cost is genuinely lower and the catalogue is
not comparable in size.

Buy packs if you work in bursts, if you resent recurring charges, or if you have found a pack
that is exactly the record you are trying to make. Paying 16 cents a sound to never think
about an allowance again is a rational trade, not a mistake.

Buy the subset, not the ZIP, when you honestly only want one part of a pack. That is the
single biggest saving available here and it takes one extra click.

What should not decide it is the phrase "royalty free". Both models use it, both mean the
same narrow thing, and neither gives you ownership of anything.
