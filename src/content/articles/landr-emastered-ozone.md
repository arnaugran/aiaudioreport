Two of these rent you a service. One sells you a plugin. That is the whole decision, and the
arithmetic is less close than the marketing implies.

LANDR Studio Essentials costs €12 a month and does not give you a single WAV master. Ozone
Elements costs €59 once and exports whatever you like, forever, because it runs on your
machine. Five months of the cheaper thing costs more than the thing you keep.

<div class="takeaways">
<h2>The short version</h2>

- Ozone Elements is €59 once. It pays for itself against LANDR Essentials in under five months, and against LANDR Pro in under three.
- LANDR Essentials at €12/mo produces MP3 only. You need Standard at €18 to get three WAVs a month.
- eMastered charges €108, €228 or €348 a year for identical features, depending only on how you pay.
- eMastered's "unlimited" distribution is capped at 20 recordings per rolling 30 days.
- All eMastered prices exclude VAT, so the figure you see is not the figure you pay.

</div>

## What each one charges

| | Price | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| LANDR Studio Essentials | €12/mo | Unlimited MP3 masters, no WAV |
| LANDR Studio Standard | €18/mo | Unlimited MP3 plus 3 WAV masters a month |
| LANDR Studio Pro | €23/mo | Unlimited WAV and HDWAV |
| eMastered, yearly upfront | €108/yr | Unlimited mastering, stem separation, distribution\* |
| eMastered, yearly billed monthly | €19/mo | Identical, with a one-year commitment |
| eMastered, rolling monthly | €29/mo | Identical |
| Ozone 12 Elements | €59 once | Plugin, includes Master Assistant |
| Ozone 12 Standard | €239 once | Plugin |
| Ozone 12 Advanced | €549 once | Plugin |

Prices read in euros on 8 November 2026. LANDR also sells annual billing, discounted heavily
at the time we looked, which we come back to below.

## eMastered charges three different prices for one product

Read those three eMastered rows again. The feature lists are not similar, they are identical:
unlimited mastering, unlimited stem separation, unlimited distribution, the ATOM synth,
PitchPro and Linkify appear on all three.

What changes is only how you pay.

- €108 a year, paid upfront
- €19 a month on an annual plan, which is €228 a year, and you are committed for the year
- €29 a month rolling, which is €348 a year

Paying monthly costs **3.2 times** what paying upfront costs, for the same software. The
middle option is the one to avoid entirely: you take the one-year commitment of an annual
plan and still pay €120 more than the annual price.

Two months free for annual billing is the ordinary pattern in this market, and we have found
it repeatedly, at Splice, at Loopcloud and at Loopmasters. A 3.2x spread is not that pattern.
It is a different kind of pricing.

Also worth knowing before you compare anything: eMastered states "Prices exclude VAT. Taxes
will be applied at checkout." LANDR and iZotope showed tax-inclusive euro prices to us. The
eMastered figures above are therefore not comparable to the others until you add your own
VAT rate.

## "Unlimited" has an asterisk, and the asterisk has a number

eMastered prints "Unlimited* Distribution" and the asterisk leads to a Fair Usage Policy. That
document is specific, which is to its credit:

> Distribution of up to 20 new recordings per rolling 30-day period.

Submissions beyond that "will be automatically blocked". The policy walks through a worked
example of the rolling window, and limits accounts to a single artist profile.

Be precise about what this does and does not cover. The cap is on **distribution**. Unlimited
mastering and unlimited stem separation carry no documented numeric limit in that policy, and
the asterisk on the pricing card sits only against distribution. If you came for mastering,
the cap is not aimed at you.

The policy also prohibits "low-effort or AI-generated spam content", which is worth reading if
you work with generators. We covered where that sits across platforms in
[Can You Upload AI Music?](/articles/can-you-upload-ai-music/).

## The break-even, which is shorter than you would guess

A plugin is a one-off cost. A subscription is a rate. So the only question that matters is how
long it takes the rate to reach the one-off.

Against LANDR Essentials, the tier that produces no WAV files at all, Ozone Elements pays for
itself in under five months and then costs nothing ever again. Against LANDR Pro it takes
under three.

The higher Ozone tiers change this, obviously. Standard at €239 needs about ten months of
LANDR Pro to break even, and Advanced at €549 needs about two years. Those are real
commitments and the case for them is not price.

## Where the subscription genuinely wins

The break-even maths is not the whole argument, and a piece that stopped there would be
misleading.

LANDR and eMastered are not only mastering. Both bundle distribution to streaming platforms,
and LANDR adds sample credits, plugins and collaboration tools. Ozone is a plugin: it masters,
and that is all it does. If you were going to pay a distributor anyway, the comparison shifts
and you should price the bundle rather than the mastering.

A subscription also requires nothing of you. Ozone runs in your DAW and expects you to drive
it, even with Master Assistant doing the first pass. LANDR takes an upload and returns a
file. For some people that difference is worth more than €59.

And LANDR's annual pricing was discounted sharply when we looked, with Studio Pro showing
€11.25 a month billed yearly against €22.50 undiscounted, labelled 50% off. A promotional rate
is not a standing rate and we would not plan around it, but if you are buying today it is real
and it roughly halves the figures above.

## What each Ozone edition actually contains

Reading iZotope's own edition comparison settles what the three prices buy,
and the answer reframes the cheapest one.

**Elements, €59.** Master Assistant, with the new Custom Flow and the Assistant
View, plus Assistive Vocal Balance. That is close to the whole list. Elements
does not give you the individual modules, so you get the chain the assistant
builds and you do not get to take it apart.

**Standard, €239.** This is where Ozone becomes a mastering suite rather than
an assistant: Equalizer, Dynamics, Dynamic EQ, Maximizer, Imager, Exciter,
Match EQ, Bass Control, Stabilizer, Master Rebalance, the four Vintage modules,
Dither and Track Referencing.

**Advanced, €549.** Adds Clarity, Impact, Low End Focus, Spectral Shaper, Stem
EQ, Unlimiter, Stem Focus, Codec Preview, the Relay bonus plugin, and the
ability to run the modules as separate plugins outside Ozone.

That changes how to read the break-even above. If you want AI mastering, which
is the thing LANDR and eMastered sell, Elements at €59 is a fair comparison
and the arithmetic holds. If you want to adjust what the assistant did, you
are comparing against Standard at €239, and the break-even against LANDR Pro
stretches to about ten months.

## What we would do

Buy Ozone Elements if what you want is the assistant. At €59 it beats every subscription here
within five months and it keeps working when you stop paying. Know what you are buying: the
assistant builds you a chain, and Elements does not let you open the individual modules to
change it. For most people comparing this against LANDR, that is the same deal LANDR offers
anyway, at a fifth of the annual cost.

Subscribe to LANDR if you want distribution and mastering from one place, and take the annual
billing while the discount is running. Essentials at €12 a month is the one to skip, because
a mastering service that will not give you a lossless file is not finishing your record. We
went through that in detail in
[LANDR Pricing](/articles/landr-pricing-unlimited-mastering/).

If you choose eMastered, pay the €108 upfront. Paying €29 a month for the identical product is
the most expensive decision available anywhere in this comparison.
