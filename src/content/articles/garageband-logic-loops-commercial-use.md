Apple's licences let you release music built from the loops that come with GarageBand and Logic Pro, royalty free, with no fee owed to Apple for each release. Where producers get caught is the other half of the same paragraph, which decides what a finished piece of music is and what is just the loops passed along in a new wrapper.

There are four documents involved, because Apple writes a separate licence for each app on each platform: GarageBand for Mac, GarageBand for iPhone and iPad, Logic Pro for Mac and Logic Pro for iPad. I read all four on 22 November 2026, as linked from Apple's software licence index. They agree on the basics and disagree in two places that matter.

## The permission they share

Every one of them says the bundled content may be used on a "royalty-free basis" to create "your own original soundtracks" for your projects, and that you may broadcast and distribute those soundtracks. The Mac Logic Pro licence names film, video and audio projects; the others say video and audio. The GarageBand licences call the content Digital Materials and list individual samples, sound sets and audio loops among them. The Logic Pro licences call it Sample Content and cast the net wider: audio files, loops, built-in sound files, impulse responses, sound settings and more.

"Soundtrack" reads oddly to a songwriter, but nothing in the clause limits it to film. It is Apple's word for the thing you made. A song on Spotify, a beat on YouTube, a podcast intro and a score for a client's video all fit, as long as the loops are part of something you created.

The Logic Pro for iPad version spells the permission out more fully than the rest. You may "broadcast, publish, display, and/or distribute" what you made.

## What "standalone" rules out

The restriction follows straight on in each licence. The loops may not be distributed commercially or otherwise "on a standalone basis", and may not be repackaged "in whole or in part" as other assets. The lists differ slightly:

| Licence | Repackaging it names |
|---|---|
| GarageBand for Mac and for iOS | audio samples, sound libraries, sound effects, music beds |
| Logic Pro for Mac and for iPad | audio samples, clipart, music beds, sound effects, sound files, sound libraries, stock animation |

"In whole or in part" is the phrase to take seriously. Chopping an Apple Loop into one-shots, resampling it through a tape plugin and selling the results as a drum kit is still repackaging part of it as audio samples. So is dropping a few Apple Loops into a free loop pack you give away to grow a mailing list, because the restriction covers distribution "commercially or otherwise", which means free counts too.

Music beds are the entry I'd watch if you do library or production music. A bed is exactly the kind of underscore a client licenses to put under a voiceover. My reading is that a bed you composed, where the loops are one layer of an arrangement, is your soundtrack, while an Apple Loop with a fade on each end is the loop sold as a bed. Apple doesn't draw that line in numbers, so I wouldn't send anything close to it to a library.

The same reasoning sorts out the beat-selling question. A finished beat leased on a marketplace is a piece of music you made, and in my reading that sits inside the permission. A "loop kit" or "melody pack" built from Apple content, sold so that other producers can make their own tracks from it, sits squarely in the prohibited list. The difference is whether the buyer gets your work or Apple's sounds.

GarageBand for Mac adds one further sentence that the Logic Pro licences lack. You may not "use, extract or distribute" any Digital Materials on a standalone basis, or use them "outside the context of its intended use as part of the Apple Software". In my reading, pulling the raw files out of the app's library folder to load into another sampler is the obvious thing that sentence is aimed at.

## The iPhone and iPad GarageBand licence bans AI training

This is the newest of the four documents, going by the file Apple serves, and it is the only one with a clause about machine learning. You may not use the Digital Materials "for training, testing, or evaluating, any machine learning or artificial intelligence models, tools, algorithms, sound generators, or other software programs."

That is broader than it first looks. As I read it, "testing or evaluating" means a developer can't use GarageBand's loops as a benchmark set for a stem separator or a drum classifier either, not only as training data. And in my reading "sound generators" catches a plain synth or sampler plugin that ships with presets derived from them.

The Mac GarageBand licence and both Logic Pro licences say nothing about AI at all. I wouldn't read that silence as permission. The standalone and repackaging clauses already sit in each of them, and feeding a library of loops into someone else's software is hard to square with using them only as part of Apple's app. If you build audio tools, assume none of this content is usable for that and source a dataset with its own written licence.

## Logic Pro for iPad and what happens when you stop paying

Logic Pro for iPad is sold as a monthly or annual subscription, and its licence ties the sounds to it. When the subscription ends, Apple says, "you will lose full access to the Apple Software, including loss of certain functionality, and Sample Content." You then get limited access, for a limited time, to "retrieve, export, and maintain access to" projects you saved.

The clause says nothing about music you already released. The royalty-free permission is written without a time limit, and the termination clause is about access to the app and its sounds, so my reading is that a song you finished and distributed while subscribed doesn't have to come down when you cancel. What you lose is the ability to keep working on projects that depend on those loops inside Logic.

That makes the export window the practical point. Before you cancel, bounce stems of any unfinished project that leans on Apple Sample Content, so the parts survive as audio. Apple gives no length for the "limited time", and I wouldn't bet a half-finished album on it being generous.

The iPad licence also reserves the right to "automatically modify, remove or delete any Sample Content" through updates, and says Apple doesn't guarantee that any specific content stays available. That applies while you are subscribed too. It's one more reason to print loops to audio in any project you care about rather than leaving them as live library references.

## The Mac Logic Pro licence is an old document

The file Apple's index links for Logic Pro on the Mac is headed "Logic Pro X Software License Agreement", and its PDF was produced in June 2013. Its sample clause matches the iPad version's list almost word for word, without the later additions about Apple removing content. If you bought Logic for Mac, that is the text Apple currently points to, and on loops it says what the others do.

## Royalty free is not the same as exclusive

Nothing in these licences makes the loops yours. Apple and its licensors keep ownership, and the GarageBand licences say title in the content belongs to its owners. Every GarageBand and Logic user has the same files.

This is where releasing gets awkward in practice. A track whose most recognisable part is an unaltered Apple Loop sounds like a lot of other people's tracks, and any rights system that matches audio can't tell your use from theirs. I covered how that plays out for non-exclusive samples in the piece on [Splice samples and YouTube Content ID](/articles/splice-samples-youtube-content-id/), and the same caution applies to Apple's library: don't register a loop-led track with a fingerprinting system as if every sound in it were exclusively yours.

My judgement on the creative side is the same as with any shared library. Loops are a fine starting point, and the licence clearly expects you to use them that way. The more you change, layer and arrange, the more plainly the result is your soundtrack and the less it shares with everyone else's.

## What to do before you release

Open the project and list which parts are Apple content. For a song, an EP or a beat you're leasing, the licence covers you, so release it. For anything that hands the sounds themselves to someone else, from a sample pack to a preset bank to a free loop download, take the Apple material out or replace it with your own recordings.

If you are on Logic Pro for iPad, bounce stems of every unfinished project before you cancel. And if your plans include training or testing an audio model, leave Apple's loops out of the dataset entirely, whichever app they came from.
