Apple's own Logic Pro page now leads with a subscription. The "Try it free" buttons all point to Apple Creator Studio, a bundle of Logic, Final Cut Pro, Pixelmator Pro and a handful of smaller apps, and the one-time purchase sits a line further down: "Logic Pro for Mac is also available as a one-time purchase on the App Store."

It's still there, at the same $199.99 the Mac App Store listed when I checked on 24 November 2026. So the question for a producer on a budget is a real one, and the answer depends on how long you expect to keep using Logic and which devices you want it on. Everything below comes from Apple's product pages, its support articles and the App Store listing, all read on that date.

## What each option costs

Apple quotes US prices, so these are in dollars. Its Creator Studio page has a link to see what the subscription costs in your region.

| Option | Price | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Logic Pro for Mac, one-time | $199.99 | Logic Pro on Mac |
| MainStage for Mac, one-time | $29.99 | MainStage on Mac |
| Creator Studio, monthly | $12.99 a month | Logic Pro and Final Cut Pro for Mac and iPad, MainStage, Motion, Compressor, Pixelmator Pro, premium features in Keynote, Pages, Numbers and Freeform |
| Creator Studio, annual | $129 a year | Same |
| Creator Studio, education | $2.99 a month or $29.99 a year | Same, for verified college students and educators |

New subscribers get a 30-day free trial. Apple's support page adds a separate offer: if you buy a new Mac or iPad, you can use Creator Studio for three months before billing starts, provided you sign up with the Apple Account you set the device up with.

On features, Apple's Logic Pro FAQ is direct about it: "Logic Pro and MainStage have all the same features whether they are subscription or one-time-purchase versions." The App Store listing for the $199.99 version describes the tools a budget producer is likely buying Logic for, including Stem Splitter separating "nearly any mixed audio recording into 11 distinct parts", Mastering Assistant and six AI Session Players. There's one exception to the same-features line, which I'll come to.

## The break-even arithmetic

On the monthly plan, $199.99 divided by $12.99 is 15.4. Fifteen months cost $194.85; the sixteenth takes you to $207.84, past the price of buying Logic outright. The annual plan is lumpier. One year costs $129, and the second payment, due at the start of month 13, takes you to $258. In cash terms that renewal is the one that puts you past the one-time price. Spread $129 over twelve months and it's $10.75 a month, which reaches $199.99 at about 18.6 months of use, so if you renew for a second year and keep Logic all the way through it, you've paid for the Mac version and then some.

Three years makes the gap plain. Annual billing comes to $387 and monthly to $467.64, against $199.99 paid once. The annual plan itself saves $26.88 a year over twelve monthly payments ($155.88).

If you're buying a new Mac anyway, the three free months push each crossover back by three months: month 19 on monthly billing, and the second annual renewal arrives in month 16.

Students are a different calculation entirely. At $29.99 a year, it would take about six and two-thirds years of subscription to match the one-time price, and most degrees end long before that. Apple asks students to reverify once a year, and its support page says that if you don't, or you've graduated, the plan "automatically renews as a standard Apple Creator Studio subscription." That's the moment the arithmetic above starts to apply to you, so it's worth a calendar note.

## What happens when you stop paying

This is the clause I'd read twice before subscribing. Apple's Creator Studio FAQ says your projects in Logic Pro "remain on all your devices, and you can copy or share them to any other device. To open or edit a project, you need to be an active subscriber."

So a cancelled subscription leaves you with Logic sessions you can see in Finder and can't open in the subscription app. Apple's support page offers two ways back: it says you keep your files "so you can work on your projects if you decide to resubscribe later or if you want to buy an app that's available as a one-time purchase."

That second route matters. If you subscribe for a while and then decide Logic is the DAW you're staying with, buying the $199.99 version is Apple's own suggested way to keep working on those projects. Apple also says both versions can be installed on the same Mac at once, and the Creator Studio apps carry their own icons so you can tell them apart.

Bounced audio is a different matter. A WAV you exported while subscribed is just a file, and nothing on Apple's pages suggests it stops playing. My advice, as judgement rather than anything Apple says: if you're on a subscription, export stems of anything finished before you cancel, so the music doesn't depend on the session.

## The one feature only subscribers get

Apple's support article on Creator Studio's intelligence features lists, among those "available exclusively to Apple Creator Studio subscribers", the assistant in Logic Pro for Mac. The same page says it's powered by Google Gemini models, available in U.S. English only, needs a Mac with Apple silicon running macOS 27 or later, and counts against a usage limit that resets each month. Subscribers who hit the limit can buy usage packs in eight countries, the US and UK among them.

That sits awkwardly beside the "all the same features" line in the Logic Pro FAQ. Both are on Apple's own pages, and I'd read the support article as the more specific of the two. Stem Splitter, Mastering Assistant, Session Players and Chord ID all appear in the App Store listing or the Logic FAQ as part of Logic Pro itself, and nothing I read ties them to the subscription. The assistant is the bit you'd be renting.

Whether that's worth anything to you is your call. For a producer who mostly needs a DAW, a stem splitter and a mastering chain, I don't think a chat assistant with a monthly cap changes the maths.

## iPad, family and the rest of the bundle

The one-time purchase is for Mac only. Apple's support page lists Logic Pro for iPad 4.0 inside Creator Studio, and Apple says the subscription includes "Logic Pro and Final Cut Pro for Mac and iPad". If you want Logic on an iPad, you're subscribing whichever way you go.

Anyone who already had the older standalone Logic Pro for iPad subscription should know that starting Creator Studio stops it auto-renewing; Apple says you can request a prorated refund for the rest of it, or renew it later if you cancel Creator Studio.

Family Sharing changes the per-person cost. A standard subscription can be shared with up to five other family members, so a household of six on the annual plan pays $21.50 each a year. Education plans can't be shared.

The bundle has to be worth something to you beyond Logic, too. If you'd otherwise buy Final Cut Pro for your release videos, or you'd use Pixelmator Pro for artwork, the subscription is carrying more than one app. If you'd open nothing but Logic, you're paying for a bundle you don't use.

Both routes need a Mac with Apple silicon, according to Apple's Logic Pro FAQ and its support page. An Intel Mac rules out both.

## Who should buy and who should rent

My view: if you work on a Mac, you only want Logic, and you expect to still be using it in a year and a half, buy it. The $199.99 is cheaper from month 16 on monthly billing and from the first annual renewal, and your sessions never depend on a renewal going through.

Rent instead if you're a verified student, because $29.99 a year is hard to argue with and you can buy the Mac app later if you need your projects after the discount ends. Rent if you want Logic on an iPad, since there's no other way to get it there. Rent if Final Cut Pro or Pixelmator Pro are on your list anyway, or if you'd share the plan with family.

If you're not sure Logic is your DAW at all, the 30-day trial costs nothing; if you decide against Apple entirely, I've compared what the [free DAWs leave out](/articles/free-daws-what-they-limit/).

Before you start that trial, open the subscription settings and check whether you've picked monthly or annual. On monthly the one-time price is behind you after 16 payments; on annual, the renewal date a year out is the one to set a reminder for.
