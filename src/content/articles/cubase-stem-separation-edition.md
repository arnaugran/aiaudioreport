Only Cubase Pro. On Steinberg's comparison table for Cubase 16, the Stem Separation row reads Yes under Pro and No under Artist, and the columns for Elements, AI and LE are left empty. Steinberg's own features page tags the entry "Pro" and nothing else.

If you bought Artist because it looked like the sensible middle, and you are now hunting for the stems command, it is not in a submenu you missed. It belongs to the $599.99 edition.

## Three editions, one with stems

I read these prices on Steinberg's comparison page on 14 November 2026. None of them were shown as discounted.

| Edition | Full version price | Stem Separation | Tracks | Effects |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cubase Pro 16 | $599.99 | Yes | Unlimited | 98 |
| Cubase Artist 16 | $299.99 | No | Unlimited | 75 |
| Cubase Elements 16 | $99.99 | Not listed | Limited | 45 |
| Cubase AI 16 | Bundled with selected Yamaha hardware | Not listed | Limited | 31 |
| Cubase LE 16 | Bundled with partner products | Not listed | Limited | 29 |

So the gap between the cheapest Cubase with stems and the edition just below it is $300.

Stems are not the only audio tool Artist misses. In the same Audio editing section, Steinberg marks Spectral Audiowarp, Audio Alignment, Direct Offline Processing, Group Editing and Modulators as Pro, and elsewhere the table puts a No against Artist and Elements for Spectral Comparison EQ. Artist does have VariAudio 3, SpectraLayers Go and WaveLab Go, which Elements does not.

## What Cubase 16 changed

Stem separation in Cubase is not new to version 16. Steinberg's "New in 16" page describes the update as "higher-quality stems, now with dedicated piano and guitar separation for even more flexible editing and remixing", and its summary list puts it as "Better quality plus piano and guitar extraction." The feature tile carries a single edition label: Pro.

Steinberg publishes no quality figures for the separation, and I have not run it, so I make no claim about how clean the stems are. The features page calls it "AI-powered separation tools", which tells you the method and nothing about the result.

## The SpectraLayers Go consolation

Artist owners are not entirely without unmixing. Cubase Artist 16 includes SpectraLayers Go, according to the same comparison table, and SpectraLayers is Steinberg's spectral editor with its own AI unmixing.

Go is the smallest version of it. On Steinberg's SpectraLayers comparison chart, song unmixing is rated Full for SpectraLayers Pro 13 and "Vocals only" for both Elements 13 and Go. The modules that split out an instrument, a drum kit, two voices or a chorus have a Yes against Pro and nothing against Go. Steinberg's own blurb for Elements describes it as having "AI-assisted vocal unmixing", which matches the table.

In practice that means an Artist owner can pull a vocal away from its backing in SpectraLayers Go, and that is as far as it goes. If an acapella or an instrumental is all you need for a remix, that may be enough, and you already own it.

If you want drums, bass and the rest on separate tracks, Go will not do it. Steinberg sells SpectraLayers Pro 13 separately at $359.99, with upgrades listed from $289.99. Compare that with the $299.99 it costs to take Artist 16 to Cubase Pro 16 and the separate app looks like the worse deal for a Cubase user, unless you also want SpectraLayers' repair tools or plan to use it in a second DAW.

## What getting to Pro actually costs

This is where Steinberg's pricing turns out to be fairer than the $300 gap suggests. I opened the upgrade dialog on Steinberg's shop for every path into Cubase Pro 16 and wrote down each price.

| You own | Price to reach Cubase Pro 16 |
|---|---|
| Cubase Pro 15 | $99.99 (update) |
| Cubase Pro 12, 13 or 14 | $199.99 (update) |
| Cubase Pro 6 to 11 | $249.99 (update) |
| Cubase Artist 16 | $299.99 |
| Cubase Artist 12 to 15 | $379.99 |
| Cubase Artist 6 to 11 | $399.99 |
| Cubase Elements 6 to 16 | $499.99 |
| Cubase AI or LE 12 to 16 | $499.99 |

Add those to the original purchase and the arithmetic is almost perfectly flat. Artist 16 at $299.99 plus the $299.99 upgrade is $599.98, one cent under Pro bought outright. Elements 16 at $99.99 plus $499.99 also comes to $599.98. Steinberg charges you the difference and nothing more for stepping up within the current version. Older Artist licences pay a little extra, and the shop shows why. Artist 15 to Artist 16 was $79.99 on the updates page, and $79.99 plus $299.99 is $379.98, which is the Artist 12 to 15 upgrade price to the cent. The extra $80 is the version update, folded in.

The one path that saves real money is the free one. If Cubase AI or LE came in the box with an interface or keyboard, $499.99 takes you to Pro 16, which is $100 less than a new Pro licence. Elements owners pay the same $499.99, but they paid for Elements first.

Two more routes appear on Steinberg's trial page. If you use another DAW, it says you "can save 40% on Cubase Pro" with a crossgrade. Students and teachers get educational offers of "up to 50% off the full retail price".

## Try Pro first, because the trial keeps your work

Steinberg's trial page offers a free 30-day trial of Cubase Pro 16, the edition with Stem Separation. It asks for a Steinberg account and, according to the FAQ, no payment details: "You can start the Cubase trial without providing any payment details."

The terms after the trial are kinder than some. Steinberg says that once the 30 days are up you need a licence to keep going, but "your projects and settings remain intact, so you can pick up right where you left off once you have purchased a full license." The FAQ also says the trial can sit alongside an older Cubase installation without affecting it.

That last point matters for the upgrade decision. An Artist 15 owner can install the Pro 16 trial next to their existing copy, run their own material through the new separation, and only then decide whether $379.99 is worth it.

There is also a separate Elements 16 trial on the same page. It will not help with this question.

## Who should choose what

If you are new to Cubase and want stems inside it, buy Pro. Nothing below Pro has the feature, and Steinberg's upgrade prices mean buying Artist now and Pro later costs the same total but leaves you without stems in between.

If you already own Artist 16 and stems are the only Pro feature you want, the $299.99 upgrade is fair arithmetic.

It is also a lot of money for one function. I would look at a standalone separator first, and I keep [a list of what stem separation costs elsewhere](/articles/ai-stem-separation-costs/), including free options. Try the vocal unmixing in the SpectraLayers Go you already own before paying for anything.

If you own Cubase AI or LE from a hardware bundle, you are the buyer Steinberg's price list favours most. $499.99 gets you Pro 16, a hundred dollars under the full price.

If you are still choosing a DAW, I have answered the same question for [FL Studio](/articles/fl-studio-stem-separation-edition/) and [Ableton Live](/articles/ableton-live-stem-separation-edition/). My view is that nobody should pick Cubase Pro for stems alone at $599.99. Pick it for the rest of what Pro includes, and treat stems as one more row on the table.
