The biggest difference shows up when you cancel. Loopcloud says unused points stay in your account; Splice says leftover credits expire 28 days after your final billing period.

If you work in bursts, that can decide it. The credit and point systems also price sounds differently, so here's the cost comparison before the verdict.

## The unit trap

Most comparisons of these two put "100 credits" next to "100 points" and call it a tie. That
is wrong, and the companies' own documentation says so.

On Splice, "All samples on Splice cost 1 credit." MIDI patterns and presets can cost up to 3.

On Loopcloud, points are priced by what the file is. The help centre is explicit: "A One Shot
(e.g. a drum hit or a single synth note) is 1 point and a Loop is usually 2 points." Longer
files cost more, and "certain files such as vocals can be priced up to 8 points."

So the headline number on a Loopcloud plan tells you what you can spend, not how many sounds
you get. If you mostly pull loops, halve it. If you pull vocal stems, divide by rather more.

Splice also runs promotional first-period pricing: $4.99 for a first month on Creator monthly,
or $10.00/mo for a first year on Creator yearly.

One mismatch worth flagging. Splice's support FAQ lists three tiers, Sounds+, Creator and
Creator+ at 100, 200 and 500 credits, while the live plans page shows two. I'm reporting
the live page and noting the discrepancy rather than blending them.

### "Save 50%" is the promo, not the annual rate

The Yearly toggle on Splice's plans page carries a "SAVE 50%" badge. That 50% is the
first-year promotional rate, $10.00 against the $19.99 monthly list. The standing yearly
price is $16.67/mo, which is a saving of about 16.6%.

Which, once you do the arithmetic, is the same deal Loopcloud offers.

### Both of them charge ten months for twelve

Worth checking rather than assuming, because [I found the opposite at
LALAL.AI](/articles/ai-stem-separation-costs/), where the annual price is exactly twelve times
the monthly one and the discount is zero.

Loopcloud: Artist is €119.40 billed monthly against €99.50 annually. Studio is €179.40
against €149.50. Professional is €275.40 against €229.50. Every gap is precisely two months of
the monthly price, and the annual point allowance is exactly twelve times the monthly one, so
you get the same thing for ten months' money.

Splice: Sounds is $155.88 billed monthly against $130.08 yearly. Creator is $239.88 against
$200.04. Both work out at a hair under two months saved.

Two companies, different currencies, different units, same underlying offer. If you are
confident you will use the service for a year, take the annual on either. If you are not,
read the next section first, because that confidence is exactly what the cancellation terms
test.

Splice has one price per sample whatever it is, which makes it predictable and makes loops
relatively cheap. Loopcloud rewards one shots and punishes vocals. A drum programmer and a
vocal-led producer will get very different value from the same plan, which is not something
either company's marketing makes obvious.

## The part that actually decides it

<div class="stats">
<div class="stat"><b>Always</b><span>Loopcloud: unspent points roll over, including after cancelling</span></div>
<div class="stat"><b>28 days</b><span>Splice: leftover credits expire after your final cancellation date</span></div>
<div class="stat"><b>2 months</b><span>Splice: the longest you can pause without losing accrued credits</span></div>
</div>

Read those together and a pattern appears. Splice is built for continuous subscribers. Credits
roll over month to month, but only while you keep paying, and the moment you stop there is a
short clock on everything you banked. Pausing protects you for up to two months, and that is
the mechanism they want you to use.

Loopcloud treats points more like a stored balance. They roll over, they survive cancellation,
and they are there if you come back.

<p class="pullquote">If you work in bursts, a studio month followed by three quiet ones, Loopcloud is holding your money and Splice is spending it.</p>

Neither approach is dishonest. They are different products. But the Splice clock is in a
support article rather than on the pricing page, and it is the single most expensive detail in
either service for an occasional user.

## What they both do well

Both are unambiguous that downloads are yours permanently. Splice says you "can still use and
download any sounds you previously purchased" after cancelling, and its plans page leads with
"Yours forever" and "100% royalty free". Loopcloud says "Every sound you purchase is yours to
keep forever, with or without a subscription."

One difference on storage: Loopcloud keeps cloud-stored content for up to 90 days after a
lapse, "after which your stored files may be deleted". Your downloads are safe, your cloud
library is not. Keep local copies.

Splice also makes a claim Loopcloud does not: "Our catalog is human-made. We will never train
AI on your work." If where the sounds come from matters to you, that is on the record and
worth weighing against the [licensing questions that follow AI-generated
material](/articles/can-you-sell-suno-music/).

## Which to pick

**You produce most weeks and pull a lot of loops.** Splice. One credit per sample is
predictable, and the rollover works fine as long as you stay subscribed.

**You produce in bursts.** Loopcloud, and probably monthly rather than annual. The points
survive the quiet months, which is worth more than any per-sound price here.

**You build drum kits from one shots.** Loopcloud, by a distance. At one point each, Studio
and Professional are the cheapest sounds in this article.

**You work mainly with vocal stems.** Check the point cost of the specific files before
subscribing. At up to 8 points, a Loopcloud allowance can empty fast, and Splice's flat credit
becomes the better deal.

**You are about to stop for a while.** Pause Splice rather than cancelling it. Two months of
protection is free and cancelling throws the balance away.
